Record types

Fourteen record types, each a separate fact

The registry keeps separate facts in separate records. A certificate is not a field on a lot. It is its own record, linked to the lot, with its own issuer, date, fingerprint and approval. One record can then be corrected, superseded or withdrawn without touching the others.

Asset programs

Cu and Ni program definitions, status and program-level documents.

Lots

Production lots, such as Cu #03-K-07 and Ni 120/NP1, with declared quantity and specification.

Batches

Sub-lot production or structuring batches where a lot is split.

Containers

Boxes and packages. This is the redemption unit for copper powder.

Coils

Bobbins and coils. This is the redemption unit for nickel wire. There are 30 bobbins in Lot 120/NP1.

Laboratories

Issuer identity, location and accreditation evidence, where it has been provided.

Certificates of Analysis

Exact transcription, assay grid, method, standard, sample record, source and fingerprint.

Custody and ownership

Custody intake, receipts, seals, chain-of-title and ownership records.

Valuations

Methodology, benchmark, currency, date, valuer and review date.

Insurance

Policy schedules and coverage periods, where arranged. None are arranged today.

Reserve reports

Reconciliation snapshots, attestation files and as-of dates.

Token programs

Proposed parameters for each program. All of them are pending, and none is hard-coded.

Redemptions

Requests, eligibility decisions, burn references and release records.

Documents

Every file, with its SHA-256 fingerprint, version and visibility level.

Unit-level registry

Every lot, container and bobbin, each with its own status line

Each row is a unit with its own passport. Verification, custody, reserve, tokenization, redemption and availability statuses change only through the four-eyes workflow, when supporting evidence is approved.

Publication control

Nothing is public until a second person approves it

Ownership, laboratory, custody, insurance and reserve claims stay unpublished until evidence is supplied and approved. The workflow enforces this. It does not depend on editorial habit.

  1. Draft

    Visible only to the editor and to roles with review rights. Never indexed and never returned by the API.

  2. Under Review

    Submitted with its evidence. The submitter cannot approve it.

  3. Approved

    A second person approves it, and the approval is bound to a SHA-256 fingerprint of the content and fields.

  4. Published

    The fingerprint is re-checked first. Any edit made after approval sends the record back to review.

  5. Unpublished or Archived

    Published records are withdrawn, never hard-deleted. Their history remains intact.

Versioning and field discipline

How the registry stays honest

Declared vs tested

Quantities and purities keep their source. The 2000 kg (Cu) and 5000 g (Ni) net weights are stored as declared by the customer, exactly as their certificates state.

Original strings preserved

Certificate values are stored as printed, for example "99,9807 %" with a comma decimal, alongside normalised numbers. The evidence view never re-renders a value differently from its source.

Pending is a value

Fields without evidence, such as particle size, tap density, tensile strength and temper, are stored and shown as "Pending". They are never left blank and never estimated.

Field-level visibility

Each field is marked public, participant, auditor or restricted. Acquisition cost and commercial terms remain restricted.

Full change history

Every transition records who made it, when, the previous and new state, and the reason. The record is held in the hash-chained audit trail.

Bulk operations, same controls

Imports and exports pass through the same workflow. A bulk import produces drafts, not published records.

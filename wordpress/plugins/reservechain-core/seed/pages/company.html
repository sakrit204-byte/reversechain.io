Mission and vision

Physical evidence first. Digital representation second.

Our mission is to build the record-keeping, verification and reconciliation infrastructure that a tokenized industrial-metal program would need before a single token could responsibly exist.

Industrial metals are traded on documents: certificates of analysis, warehouse receipts, packing lists, title chains. Those documents are usually PDFs that cannot be checked against each other, against the physical material, or against any digital claim made about it. ReserveChain is designed to bind each lot, container and coil to its evidence, so that every status shown publicly can be traced to a document, a fingerprint and an approval.

Our vision is a platform where an industrial participant, an institution or an asset owner can inspect what is known about a specific physical unit, see what is still pending, and understand exactly which independent party is expected to confirm it.

Core principles

Six rules that shape every page and every record

These principles are enforced in the software, not only stated in copy. Where a rule is technical, the mechanism is named.

Evidence before claims

No status is shown as confirmed until the supporting document has been supplied, fingerprinted and approved. Empty fields stay visibly empty: "Pending" is an honest answer.

Proposed until proven

Verification, custody, Proof of Reserves, tokenization and redemption are described as proposed frameworks, subject to final legal, contractual, technical and operational confirmation.

Unit-level traceability

Records are kept per lot, container and coil, not per program. A copper box or a nickel bobbin has its own identifier and its own Digital Asset Passport.

Separation of duties

The person who submits content can never approve it. Approval is bound to a SHA-256 fingerprint of the exact content, and any later edit returns it to review.

Tamper-evident history

Every material action is written to an append-only, hash-chained audit trail that the database itself refuses to modify. See Governance.

Restraint by default

Sensitive modules (wallet, token acquisition, holdings, redemption) are built but locked. They cannot switch on automatically and require written authorization and a deployment action.

Platform thesis

The missing layer is not the token. It is the record.

The problem

Specialist industrial materials (ultrafine powders, fine wire, high-purity grades) carry value that depends on documents few people can read and fewer can check. A digital claim about such material is only as good as the evidence trail beneath it.

The approach

Start with the evidence: transcribe certificates exactly, fingerprint every file, identify every physical unit, and expose what is pending as clearly as what is documented. Only then design issuance and redemption around that record.

The leverage

The registry, passport, audit and reconciliation components are program-agnostic. Once proven on two narrow, well-documented programs, the same infrastructure is intended to serve further approved asset classes and enterprise clients.

Expansion model and positioning

Grow by admission criteria, not by announcement

Further asset categories (precious metals, gemstones, energy assets, real estate, art and collectibles) are shown only as future, pre-launch concepts. A category becomes a program only when it passes the same gates as copper and nickel.

Admission gates for any new program

  • Documented ownership and chain of title
  • Independent laboratory or inspection evidence
  • Independent valuation methodology
  • Custody and, where applicable, insurance arrangements
  • Marketability and a defined redemption unit
  • Legal review of the token and offering structure

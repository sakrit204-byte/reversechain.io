Program overview and asset thesis

Small mass, high specification

The whole lot weighs 5000 g, as declared. Its interest lies in the specification rather than the tonnage. Nickel drawn to 25 micrometres is a precision product. Diameter consistency, surface condition and trace chemistry decide whether it can be used in sensors, fine interconnects or medical components.

The material comes on discrete bobbins, so the proposed program treats each bobbin as its own controlled unit. It would have its own identifier, passport, custody record and redemption path. Any future token program would be subject to the final Swiss corporate and issuance structure, which is in development.

  • One lot (120/NP1), one box, 30 bobbins and one certificate (No. 0004368)
  • Four bobbins (nos. 8, 10, 19 and 27) were sampled for the assay
  • No token, price, ratio or allocation is defined or implied

Material characteristics and quality parameters

How thin is 0.025 mm?

Twenty-five micrometres is roughly a quarter to a third of a typical human hair. Hair diameter varies widely, and about 70–100 µm is common. Nominal values are drawn to scale below. No product image on this site may show the wire at a misleading scale.

What the certificate does and does not show

  • The chemistry of a composite sample from four named bobbins
  • A GOST 2179-75 impurity sum of 0,0193 %, and a non-radioactive statement
  • The lot identity, the bobbin count and the nominal diameter designation
  • It does not give a per-bobbin weight, length or measured diameter.
  • It does not show ownership, the current location or custody.
  • It does not give tensile, elongation, temper or surface finish.
  • It is not an independent verification by ReserveChain.

Industrial applications and use cases

Where ultrafine nickel wire is used

This is general industry context. It is not a claim about any customer, offtake or contract. Whether this lot suits a given use depends on the tolerance and mechanical data that are still pending.

Precision electronics

Fine leads, heating elements and connections in compact components, where nickel resists corrosion.

Sensors

Temperature-sensing and hot-wire elements that rely on nickel's predictable resistance behaviour.

Battery interconnects

Research and prototyping of fine interconnects and tabs in cell assemblies.

Medical devices

Fine-wire components in instruments and devices. These need full material traceability and regulatory qualification.

Aerospace R&D

Instrumentation and test articles where a documented chemistry is required.

Filtration and catalysis research

Fine meshes and high-surface-area nickel structures in laboratory and pilot work.

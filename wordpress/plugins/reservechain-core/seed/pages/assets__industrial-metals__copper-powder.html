Program overview and asset thesis

Why a six-nines copper lot needs an evidence trail

Electrolytic copper is a commodity. At 99.9999 % ("6N") it becomes a specialty material, and its worth depends on its documents: the assay, the lot identity, how it is packed and whether each container can be traced. A buyer of high-purity powder needs proof that the material in a given box is the material that was tested.

The proposed ReserveChain program sets out to record that proof. Each container would be identified, linked to its lot and certificate, reconciled against custody records and represented by a Digital Asset Passport. Only after that could a token program be considered. Any such program would be subject to the final Swiss corporate and issuance structure, which is in development.

  • One lot (#03-K-07) and one certificate (No. 0004512) are on file.
  • The program would be controlled at container level, not as an undivided pool.
  • No token, price, ratio or allocation is defined or implied.

CoA 0004512 covers none of these. Each one stays Pending until an approved laboratory measures it on sampled containers.

What the certificate does and does not show

  • The chemical purity of a 10 g sample from one box
  • Natural isotopic composition and a non-radioactive statement
  • The lot identity and the packaging description
  • It does not establish the weight or count of the whole lot. The weight is customer-declared.
  • It does not show ownership, the current location or custody.
  • It does not give particle size, morphology, density, O2 or moisture.
  • It is not an independent verification by ReserveChain.

Industrial applications and use cases

Where high-purity copper powder is used

This is general industry context. It is not a claim about any customer, offtake or contract. Whether this lot suits a given use depends on the physical parameters that are still pending.

Electronics

Conductive layers, terminations and interconnect materials, where trace impurities affect conductivity and reliability.

Conductive pastes and inks

Printed and screen-printed conductors. Particle size and oxide content strongly affect sintering behaviour.

Energy storage

Research into current collectors, electrode additives and thermal paths in battery and capacitor systems.

Additive manufacturing and powder metallurgy

Copper parts for heat exchange and induction. These depend on particle morphology and flow, which are not yet characterised.

Research and reference use

Laboratory work that needs documented high purity and a natural isotopic composition.

Thermal management

Composite and sintered heat-spreading materials where copper's thermal conductivity is the main property.

Scope of this page

A proposed framework, stated as such

This page describes a proposed verification framework, subject to final legal, contractual, technical and operational confirmation. No independent laboratory has been appointed by ReserveChain. No unit on this site has been independently verified under this framework.

The distinction that matters

Owner-supplied is not the same as verified

A certificate supplied by an asset owner may be accurate. It is still a claim made by an interested party about a sample they arranged. ReserveChain records the source of every certificate and shows it on every passport.

Owner-supplied evidence

  • Commissioned by the owner or a prior holder
  • The sample and its chain of custody were not witnessed by ReserveChain
  • Quantities are often "according to the customer", as on both IGAS certificates
  • Displayed as an exact transcription with the original scan
  • Status shown: Pending verification

Independently verified evidence (proposed)

  • Commissioned under contract by the platform, not by the owner
  • Samples drawn by the laboratory or a witnessed inspector, then sealed
  • Tested by an accredited laboratory using a stated method and reference standard
  • The issuer confirms the certificate directly
  • The "verified" status may only be shown once an approved record exists

Workflow

Seven steps from submission to status

  1. Document intake

    The owner submits existing certificates, specifications and packing lists. Each file is fingerprinted on upload and kept unchanged.

    [rc_status s="in_development"]

  2. Document validation

    Certificate numbers, dates, lot references and signatures are checked with the issuing laboratory. Any mismatch is recorded as an exception and the certificate is not used.

    [rc_status s="proposed"]

  3. Physical inspection

    An inspector confirms packaging, labels, seal numbers and unit counts against the registry, then weighs and photographs the units.

    [rc_status s="proposed"]

  4. Sampling

    Samples are drawn under the sampling plan below. The sample containers are sealed, the seal numbers recorded, and the samples sent to the laboratory under a chain-of-custody form.

    [rc_status s="proposed"]

  5. Testing

    The laboratory runs the agreed analysis, for example ICP-MS or ICP-OES for trace elements, plus physical tests appropriate to the material form, and issues a certificate.

    [rc_status s="proposed"]

  6. Review and approval

    A reviewer compares the results with the declared specification. A second person approves the verification record, and the approval is bound to the record's fingerprint.

    [rc_status s="in_development"]

  7. Status published

    The unit's passport shows the verification record, the certificate, its fingerprint and the date by which re-verification is due.

    [rc_status s="in_development"]

Accreditation required

Any laboratory used for independent verification must hold accreditation to ISO/IEC 17025 for the methods it applies. This is a requirement on the laboratory, not a claim about ReserveChain.

Independence required

The laboratory must have no commercial relationship with the asset owner for the unit under test, and it is engaged and paid by the platform.

None appointed

No laboratory has been appointed. Partner names appear only after a contract is signed and the partner has authorized the use of its name.

Chain of evidence and re-verification

Evidence that stays attached to the unit

Each verification record links to the exact unit it covers, the certificate file, the sampling record and the person who approved it. Replacing a document creates a new version and a new fingerprint. The previous version stays in the history.

  • Scheduled re-verification: a due date is set per program, and when it passes the passport shows the record as stale.
  • Event-driven re-verification: required after a broken seal, a custody transfer, a weight discrepancy or a damaged package.
  • Pre-redemption check: the specific container or bobbin is confirmed against its record before release.
  • Valuation review: each valuation is tied to the verified specification. If the specification changes, the valuation is reviewed again.

Live tool

Verify a document fingerprint

Drop in any document you received that claims to come from ReserveChain. Your browser computes its SHA-256 fingerprint locally, and the file is never uploaded. The tool then checks the fingerprint against the published registry. A match shows the file is byte-for-byte identical to the published version. It does not, on its own, show that the content is true.

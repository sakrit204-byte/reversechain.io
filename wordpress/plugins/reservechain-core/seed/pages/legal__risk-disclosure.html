Scope

Read this first

This disclosure describes risks connected with the ReserveChain project and with any future digital representation of the industrial metals it registers. The list is not exhaustive. Risks that are unknown today, or that look immaterial now, could also affect the project.

No token is offered or sold through this website. This disclosure does not make anything an offer. If definitive offering documentation is ever issued, it would contain its own risk factors, and those would prevail.

Part A

Project and evidence risks

Development stage

Development-stage risk

ReserveChain is in development. Its features, architecture, programs and timing may change, may be delayed or may never be completed. The project may lack the funding, personnel or counterparties to continue. Completing one roadmap phase does not mean the next phase will follow.

Entity and structure risk

The proposed Swiss corporate and issuance structure has not been formed or authorised. It may never be formed. It may be formed in a different form or jurisdiction, or it may be unable to obtain the authorisations it needs. Until then, no entity stands behind statements on this website as an incorporated operator.

Owner-supplied evidence risk

The current program information rests on documents supplied by the asset owner. That includes the purities, lot numbers, declared quantities, packaging and the two IGAS Certificates of Analysis. These have not yet been confirmed with the issuing laboratory or tested independently. Copies could be incomplete, outdated, altered or wrong. A matching SHA-256 fingerprint shows only that a file is identical to the one registered. It does not show that its content is true.

Program-change risk

Programs may be changed, suspended, replaced or withdrawn. Reasons include verification results, counterparty availability, legal advice or commercial factors. Program parameters, including any proposed discount methodology, are subject to approval and may never be adopted.

Part B

Physical-asset risks

The metal itself

Asset authenticity and specification

Material may not match its described specification or provenance. Assay results can differ between methods, laboratories and samples. A certificate covers the sample analysed, which may not represent every unit in a lot. For example, four of thirty nickel bobbins are recorded as sampled. Fine powders can oxidise or absorb moisture. Ultrafine wire can deform, break or corrode. Storage and handling conditions affect quality over time.

Valuation risk

No valuation is published. Specialised forms such as 6N copper powder and 0.025 mm nickel wire have narrow markets. Their realisable value may differ sharply from any reference or benchmark price, and from the cost of production or acquisition. Any future valuation would depend on method, timing and the independence of the valuer.

Custody risk

No custodian has been appointed. Under any future arrangement, material could be lost, stolen, misallocated, commingled, damaged or affected by the custodian's fraud, error or insolvency. Segregation and the right to recover material would depend on contract terms and on local law.

Insurance risk

No insurance arrangement exists. Insurance may be unavailable or too costly. It may be insufficient, subject to exclusions or deductibles, or the insurer may dispute or fail to pay a claim.

Logistics risk

Transport, export controls, customs, dangerous-goods rules for fine powders, packaging integrity and delivery logistics can delay, raise the cost of or prevent physical movement. Material could be damaged in transit.

Part C

Market, counterparty and redemption risks

Markets and counterparties

Market and liquidity risk

No market for any ReserveChain token exists, and none is represented or promised. If a token were ever issued, it might not be transferable, or might be transferable only between eligible addresses. A holder might be unable to dispose of it at all. Any price could differ from the value of the underlying material. The underlying metals are subject to price volatility, and two programs concentrate exposure in two metals and two forms.

Counterparty risk

The project will depend on laboratories, custodians, insurers, attestors, KYC providers, logistics firms, technology vendors and banks, none of which has been appointed. Any of them could fail to perform, become insolvent, withdraw, be sanctioned or act fraudulently. Suitable counterparties may not be available on acceptable terms.

Redemption risk

Redemption is a proposed feature. It is disabled, and no redemption right exists. If it is ever introduced, it may be subject to minimum quantities, fees, delays, suspension, jurisdictional limits, delivery constraints and repeat compliance screening. It may also be unavailable for particular units or locations.

Token-to-asset linkage risk

The legal relationship between any token and specific physical units has not been determined. A token might not confer ownership, a claim on the units, or any enforceable right. Rights may be uncertain or unenforceable in some jurisdictions or in insolvency. Operational errors could cause the registry, reserve reports and on-chain state to diverge. An attestation speaks to one point in time only.

Part D

Technology risks

Contracts, networks and systems

Smart-contract risk

The contracts may contain vulnerabilities despite automated tests and the use of established libraries. No independent audit has been performed. Administrative functions such as pausing, freezing, minting and configuration create a dependency on the people who hold those roles. A compromised attestor key could post incorrect data. Staleness windows and multisig oversight reduce that risk but do not remove it.

Blockchain risk

Public blockchains can suffer congestion, fee spikes, forks, reorganisations, client defects, validator misconduct, protocol changes or attacks. Test networks may be reset or deprecated. Transactions are generally irreversible.

Wallet and key risk

Anyone who loses or exposes a private key or seed phrase may permanently lose access to any token. A malicious approval or signature request can drain a wallet. Losing or compromising administrative or multisig keys could disrupt the system. ReserveChain will never ask for your keys or seed phrase.

Platform and data risk

The website, CMS, API, portals and mobile application could have vulnerabilities, outages, dependency compromises or data loss. Restoring from backup could lose recent records. Impersonation, phishing and fake documents are a continuing risk. See the Anti-Fraud Notice.

Part E

Legal, regulatory and tax risks

Law and jurisdiction

Regulatory classification risk

Depending on structure and jurisdiction, a future token could be classed as a security, financial instrument, collective investment scheme, deposit, commodity derivative, payment instrument or other regulated product. That would bring licensing, prospectus, conduct and reporting obligations, which could make the proposed activities impossible or uneconomic. No regulatory authorisation has been obtained or is claimed.

Jurisdiction risk

ReserveChain does not currently intend to offer tokens to residents of, or persons located in, the EU/EEA. Other jurisdictions may be restricted after legal analysis, and sanctioned jurisdictions will be blocked. Eligibility rules may change, and a previously eligible person could become ineligible. Laws on crypto-assets, tokenised instruments, AML, sanctions and data protection keep changing. They could restrict or prohibit the project.

Tax risk

The tax treatment of registering interest, holding, transferring or redeeming any token, or receiving physical metal, is uncertain and differs by jurisdiction. It may include income, capital, transfer, value-added or customs taxes. Treatment may change, possibly with retrospective effect. ReserveChain gives no tax advice.

Sanctions and AML risk

Participants, counterparties or sourcing could become subject to sanctions or AML findings. That could lead to frozen addresses, exclusion, suspended programs or reporting to authorities.

Before you make any decision: read the whitepaper, the Terms of Use and, if they are ever issued, the definitive offering documents. Obtain independent legal, tax and financial advice. Do not rely on statements made outside reservechain.io.
